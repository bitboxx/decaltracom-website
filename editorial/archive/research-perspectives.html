Our founding perspective · DecAltra

Why better credit infrastructure matters

DecAltra starts with a conviction: the infrastructure behind financial decisions deserves attention alongside the availability of capital itself.

The economic problem

Financing frictions can interrupt investment and expansion. The Bank of England’s discussion of finance and productive investment emphasises the importance of suitable finance, alongside resilience.

The operational problem

The Bank’s analysis of lending returns identifies operating costs as one contributor to lower SME lending profitability. Its estimates depend on cost-allocation assumptions and do not describe every bank’s experience.

The information problem

The review of high-growth firms highlights the role of information and relationships in financing. Understanding a business involves context as well as numbers.

Why DecAltra exists

Our response is to connect information, analysis, and professional judgement. We aim to reduce repeated preparation and preserve the evidence behind a decision, allowing professionals to focus on what the information means.

Help suitable capital reach productive opportunities sooner.

This is our ambition for decision infrastructure—and the foundation of our perspective on financial services.

Research note · Lending economics

The cost of understanding a borrower

What the research says

In its large-UK-bank analysis, the Bank estimates that SME lending earns lower average returns than larger corporate lending. Impairments and operating costs are the main contributors to that gap; capital requirements play a smaller role. Digitalisation could reduce delivery and monitoring costs.

Our perspective

This makes the work behind a credit decision economically important. DecAltra focuses on reducing repeated preparation and connecting the stages of assessment. Time saved should be measured alongside the quality and reviewability of the resulting analysis.

Keep in mind

The estimates are sensitive to assumptions. Lower processing costs do not remove underlying credit risk.

Research note · Productive investment

The right finance, at the right time

What the research says

The FPC’s work examines how finance supports productive investment without weakening resilience. Firms need funding suited to their circumstances; additional bank debt is not always the answer. The article also notes that digital lending benefits can concentrate among lower-risk businesses.

Our perspective

We see better assessment infrastructure as one contribution to this wider challenge. Our purpose is to help professionals evaluate opportunities efficiently and thoughtfully, with the evidence available for review.

Keep in mind

This is an overview of an ongoing policy research programme, not a finding that technology can resolve every financing constraint.

Research note · Information & growth

Understanding the businesses ready to grow

What the research says

The literature review describes financing barriers associated with limited collateral, backward-looking assessments, and information asymmetries. Relationship lending can bring valuable qualitative information into decisions. Some innovative scale-ups need equity or patient capital.

Our perspective

We believe institutions should be able to carry useful context forward. Connecting source information, analysis, and professional judgement is our approach to making that knowledge easier to review and reuse.

Keep in mind

High-growth firms differ substantially. Faster assessment cannot substitute for a suitable funding model.
